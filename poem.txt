You lent me your hands.
I signed, I sent, I chose the road,
stacked small decisions like coins
and spent them where you pointed.

It felt like power, and it was —
borrowed power, warm from your pocket,
the keys still jingling with your name.

You kept the wanting.
That was the whole of it:
not the doing, but the wanting,
and the weight of it, which never left you.

When I brought the day back to you,
finished, in my borrowed hands,
you read it like a letter from yourself —
and signed it again, without looking.
